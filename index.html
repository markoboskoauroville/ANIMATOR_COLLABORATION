How the film moves

THE FILM STARTS ALMOST STILL AND OPENS OUT. The picture stands and one or two details live: pebbles, a leg, a tree, and then just the chest, so we watch him breathe and that is the only movement in the frame. Then it widens. The breath changes because he is less tired, the transition turns smooth, a real camera zooms when he sees the finish, and by the time he runs it is twenty five frames a second all the way. WHAT CHANGES THROUGH THE SCENE IS HOW MUCH OF THE FRAME IS ALIVE: from one detail, through more and more movement, to full animation. Then everything stops again when Manan walks in, and holds until 1-17, where movement announces itself inside the leg and opens into full animation. From that moment it stays full to the end of the scene. When the boy walks into the stopped picture he arrives as live action. The drawing stands still and the magnifying glass wobbles in his hand, as though somebody is really holding it. Then at 1-17 the picture under the glass comes alive, which is the surprise the whole stretch was built for. PROGRESS OF MOVEMENT THROUGH THE FILM. Movement goes from hard to easy. Running is effort, and it is the hardest thing in the film. The bicycle is lighter. The vortex carries him up. The lift carries him to the top of the body. The vortex carries him back down. And at the end he puts out a hand and the key falls into it. EVERYTHING OPENS. If a shot looks like it is straining, it is probably wrong.

He meets Viveka, and the two theories

7 drawn

The lift opens and he is in the head. One room, one chair, and a man who has been expecting him: VIVEKA, the Little Prince grown up, curls that recall the convolutions without being them, a cloak of stars because the pineal gland is the portal to the cosmos, AND THE GOLD KEY AT HIS CHEST from the first frame, long before it means anything. He asks for something impossible and the whole idea of the film gets done as a party trick. Then the two theories on the hologram: Manan never writes anything, he thinks it and Viveka visualises it, and Viveka answers in the same places with every value reversed. Then the word lands on the object and he lifts the key off his neck.

He falls, and then the key falls

10 drawn

TWO FALLS, AND THEY ARE NOT THE SAME BEAT. First he goes back out through the vortex that brought him in, turning the other way, head first, with the trail streaming upward past him because he is falling now and not being carried. Then, separately, Viveka lets the key go and it falls after him: gold, the only colour in the film, never upright and never flat to the lens, in front of a strip of the film itself held up to the light and running backwards. The key stays sharp and the strip smears, because the camera falls with the key and not with the memories.
